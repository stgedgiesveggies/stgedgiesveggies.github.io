Scallions

Scallions are one of those quiet workhorses that show up in nearly every week's harvest. Fresh-pulled bunches with tender green tops and mild white bottoms. Chop and drop them on a salad, whisk into a dressing, stir into eggs, or slice thin over noodles. We sometimes overwinter them so they're one of the first things ready in spring.

Flavor Mild, oniony, fresh
Texture Crisp, tender
Season Spring through Fall
Pairs with Sesame · Miso · Eggs · Lime

Recipes

15 min Makes about 1 1/2 cups Medium

Sichuan Dressing

↓ See recipe ↑ Hide recipe
Ingredients
  • —1 teaspoon Sichuan peppercorns
  • —4 scallions, thinly sliced
  • —4 garlic cloves, finely chopped
  • —2 tablespoons white or black sesame seeds
  • —1 1/2 teaspoons crushed red pepper flakes
  • —1 cup vegetable oil (grapeseed recommended)
  • —1/2 cup tamari soy sauce
  • —3 tablespoons balsamic vinegar
Method
  1. 1 Grind the peppercorns and add to a heatproof serving bowl along with the scallions, garlic, sesame seeds, and red pepper flakes.
  2. 2 Heat oil in a small heavy saucepan until you see the first sign of smoke. Carefully pour the hot oil into the bowl with the peppercorn mixture (caution: oil will bubble and rise for a second). This releases the flavors without burning them.
  3. 3 Stir in tamari and balsamic vinegar. Let cool.
  4. 4 Serve over cold noodles, steamed or stir-fried vegetables, or just about anything crunchy and hearty.
