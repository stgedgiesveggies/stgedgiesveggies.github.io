First Saturday of the season. The St. Paul Farmers' Market opens rain or shine and this year it opened in 38 degrees and a wind that came straight off the river. Sold out of chard by 9am anyway.

Spring at a market garden is a strange season. Everything is small and cold-weather and most people are still thinking about soup. But that's exactly when the best stuff is here — the greens are sweet from the frost, the roots are dense, and nothing has had time to get tired or bitter. Cold-weather vegetables taste like they're trying to prove something.

This year I'm starting with butterhead lettuce, rainbow chard, spring garlic, and the first of the radishes. Watermelon radishes are still sizing up — give them another two weeks. Asparagus crowns went in their third season, so if the math works out, we'll have those in late April.

Come find us at stall #14, east end. I'll be the one in the apron that used to be green.
