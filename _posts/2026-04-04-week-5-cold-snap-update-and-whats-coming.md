No market table this Saturday — the late cold snap we got mid-March knocked back the cold frames more than we expected, and we don't have enough to bring a worthy showing. We'd rather skip a week than show up with a half-table of sad spinach. Honesty from the farm.

## What Happened

We had one really cold night — about -8°F with wind — on March 14th. Most of our cold-hardy greens were fine under the frames, but the outer edges of two beds got some frost damage and we lost maybe a third of the crop. The rest is recovering well, and by the time you're reading this the beds are already showing new growth.

This kind of setback is normal for early-season growing in Minnesota. We build in buffer time for exactly this reason. The plants are tougher than they look.

## What's Coming

We expect to be back at market in two weeks with:

* **Spring mix and baby spinach** — recovering well, should be ready to cut
* **Arugula** — seeded a new bed last week, slightly behind but looking good
* **Tatsoi** — the most cold-tolerant of the bunch; this one barely flinched

Longer out (end of April into May): radishes, pea shoots, and the Salanova lettuce heads we're really excited about this year. We trialed Muir and Flashy Trout's Back last season and they were a hit, so we scaled both up considerably.

## A Note on the Season

We're a small operation and the weather runs the schedule, not us. We try to communicate early when things change — we'd rather you know ahead of time than show up and find an empty spot. Thanks for your patience and for following along.

See you the first Saturday in April.

— Edgie
